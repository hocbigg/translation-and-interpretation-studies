Create your own subtitle files

  • TED Translators: Get Started - The official onboarding guide for volunteer subtitlers, detailing transcription, interlingual subtitling, and review workflows on CaptionHub and Amara.
  • Quickstart Guide to Amara Subtitle Editor (US National Archives) - A practical step-by-step walkthrough covering subtitle entry, timecode synchronization, line breaks, and video caption export.
  • BBC Subtitle Guidelines - The comprehensive technical specification defining reading speeds, line lengths, display durations, and syntactic segmentation.
  • Netflix Timed Text Style Guide: General Requirements - The commercial industry benchmark specifying character limits, shot-change rules, dual-speaker dashes, and dialogue compression.

Translate your own Wikipedia articles

  • Wikipedia: Content Translation Tool Guide - The official Wikimedia manual on using the machine-assisted Content Translation environment to adapt cross-lingual wiki entries with synchronized references and infoboxes.
  • How to Translate a Wikipedia Article (wikiHow) - A visual walkthrough detailing source article selection, account configuration, target wiki formatting, and manual post-editing.
  • Wikipedia: Translation Guidelines and Best Practices - The community-consensus policy outlining attribution requirements, licensing compliance, neutral point of view preservation, and machine translation post-editing standards.

Produce your own humanitarian translations

  • A Beginner's Guide to Translating Global Voices Stories (Global Voices) - A step-by-step editorial guide for translating citizen journalism articles, focusing on journalistic style, local context glossing, and editorial review.
  • Translators without Borders Volunteer Translator Onboarding (CLEAR Global) - The orientation guide and platform portal for translating vital crisis relief information, healthcare instructions, and emergency communications for NGOs.
  • Kiva Review and Translation Team Guide - A structured procedural guide for translating and editing real microloan borrower profiles from global field partners into clear, compliant English.

Build your own terminology databases

  • How to Build a Termbase From Scratch (TCLoc Master's / University of Strasbourg) - A practitioner guide to designing concept-oriented termbases, establishing granular data categories, setting entry statuses, and adhering to ISO standards.
  • Terminology Management for Translation: A Starter Guide (COTSOES) - The official inter-institutional guide from the Conference of Translation Services of European States on compiling, structuring, and maintaining institutional multilingual termbases.
  • OmegaT Documentation and Glossary Tutorial (OmegaT) - The official technical manual on generating, formatting, and integrating plain-text tab-delimited and TBX glossary files within an open-source CAT workflow.

Execute your own software localization projects

  • Introduction to Localization (Localization Academy) - A structured open course with hands-on assignments guiding learners through auditing a live product's localization readiness, managing file formats, and executing translation workflows.
  • Software Translation Workflows (Weblate Documentation) - A comprehensive open documentation guide explaining string extraction, internationalization variable handling, plural forms, and continuous localization pipelines.

Build your own translator portfolios

  • How to Create a Localization Portfolio (POEditor Blog) - A strategic walkthrough for curating translation and localization samples, drafting problem-solution case studies, and presenting UI adaptations without breaching non-disclosure agreements.
  • Getting Started in Game Localization: Building a Portfolio (Loc'd and Loaded) - An expert game localizer's detailed guide to assembling transcreation samples, mock game tests, and specialized glossaries to demonstrate subject-matter expertise.

Write your own comparative translation analyses

  • How to Write a Comparative Analysis (Harvard College Writing Center) - The classic methodological guide on framing points of comparison, structuring thesis arguments, and alternating between text-by-text and point-by-point organizational schemes.
  • Tips for Reviewers: Comparative Analysis (Reading in Translation) - An applied essay demonstrating how to systematically compare divergent translations of the same source passage, highlighting prosody, lexical shifts, and translator stance.

Write your own translation commentaries

  • Writing a Translation Commentary by Penélope Johnson (Google Books) - [Paid book] The definitive pedagogical guide dedicated to drafting reflective academic commentaries, covering theoretical contextualization, skopos justification, and translation problem categorization.
  • Tips for Reviewers: Translators' Introductions Are Your Cheatsheet (Reading in Translation) - An analytical guide examining how published translators explain their theoretical strategies, historical constraints, and stylistic decisions in formal introductions.

Conduct your own translation quality assessments

  • Multidimensional Quality Metrics (MQM) Definition and Error Typology (W3C) - The open, international framework defining standardized hierarchical categories and severity levels (minor, major, critical) for measuring translation and post-editing quality.
  • MT Post-editing Guidelines and Quality Benchmarks (TAUS) - The industry reference defining error evaluation criteria, difference thresholds between light and full post-editing, and measurable translation quality metrics.

Compile your own conference interpreter dossiers

  • Practical Guide for Professional Conference Interpreters: Preparing for the Conference (AIIC) - The official AIIC practitioner handbook on gathering source documentation, structuring domain briefings, building ad-hoc meeting glossaries, and aligning with organizers.
  • Speech Repository: Search Speeches (European Commission) - An extensive searchable database of over 4,000 real conference and training speeches organized by language, subject domain, and difficulty to prepare thematic mock dossiers against.
  • Online Resources for Conference Interpreter Training (ORCIT) - An interactive training platform offering pedagogical modules on conference preparation, background research, and terminology activation.

Record your own consecutive interpreting performances

  • SCICtrain: Training Modules for Interpreting Students (European Commission DG Interpretation) - A comprehensive open video training curriculum demonstrating consecutive interpreting techniques, active listening, split attention, and self-evaluation protocols.
  • Note-taking for Consecutive Interpreting: A Short Course by Andrew Gillies (Google Books) - [Paid book] The standard practical coursebook on Rozan-style vertical notation, abbreviation systems, margin utilization, and self-auditing recorded consecutive deliveries.
